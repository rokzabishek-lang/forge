# How real ads cut — Apple, Nike, social ads, luxury, weddings

*Researched 2026-09-25 against Forge's five recipes (`src/shared/director/recipes/index.ts`).
Five researchers read the web (trade press, agency case pages, platform creative data,
film-studies data, wedding-film guides); a fact-checker per angle then re-opened every
cited page and struck what the page does not say. Only what survived is used below.
Pages only — no video was downloaded — so nothing here is a frame-by-frame measurement
of our own; where a number is ours, it says so.*

**The short version.** The research confirms most of the recipes' *direction* and almost
none of their *numbers* — real ads are rarely published at the level of seconds per shot,
and the precise figures that do circulate are where the invention was (below). Three
things are strong enough to act on, and they point the same way as rules this project
already wrote down from its own measurements:

1. **The fast recipes cut far faster than any verified reference.** The fastest documented
   montage found is about 2.5 s per visible cut; our Energy ad reaches a cut every beat
   (0.48 s at 124 BPM), which our own cut planner calls frantic.
2. **Pacing is in beats, so shot length depends on the song's tempo.** Our own earlier
   research says cut density should not. The same wedding ad cuts 1.6× faster on a 140 BPM
   song than on a 90 BPM one.
3. **Luxury and perfume ads barely cut at all.** Fashion/perfume should hold longer, not
   montage slowly.

And one scope question for weddings: the genre's short form is a 60–90 s teaser, and the
Director's default ad is 30 s.

## What held up

### Apple

- **Short product reveals are 30 s and carry no licensed song** — both *iPhone 15 Pro —
  Titanium* (2023) and *Apple Watch Ultra — Call to the Wild* (2022), per iSpot's ad
  database. Sound design or voice-over carries the reveal. *(data, two ads)*
- ***Titanium* is one continuous build, not a montage**: raw titanium falls, becomes the
  frame, and settles on a static close-up with the tagline over it — no black gap before
  that card is described. *(9to5mac)*
- **The reveal grammar**, from guides that reverse-engineer Apple's tech films: a slow,
  backlit hero reveal, then details, then the product in context — one build, not many
  cuts. *(Manfrotto; expert guidance)*
- ***1984*** (60 s): the climax is one hit-like beat (the hammer), then near-silence, one
  black text card read aloud, a fade, then the logo. *(Wikipedia)*
- Long emotional films (*Dear Apple*, ~3 min; *Misunderstood*; *Welcome Home*, ~4 min, its
  turn about 63 % through) are chains of vignettes with the turn placed late — a different
  form from the reveal, and not what the Director makes.

### Nike

- ***You Can't Stop Us*** (2020): 90 s, **36 split-screen pairs** built from 72 shortlisted
  clips (from an archive of ~4,000) — **about 2.5 s per visible cut**. It reads as fast
  through matched framing across the split, not cut speed. *(itsnicethat, Digital Synopsis)*
- ***Dream Crazy*** (2018): a two-minute film, voice-over led, a sequence of athlete
  vignettes; no identified song. *Find Your Greatness* (2012): one locked-off shot, no cuts,
  quiet sound, a voice-over. *(Adweek, W+K, Lürzer's Archive, Mediashower)*
- The anthem films carry their message by voice and keep on-screen type to **one closing
  line** — an absence in the coverage rather than a count, but consistent across three films.

### Short-form social ads (platform data — the most reliable tier)

- **Title in the first 2–2.5 s**: +48 % recall, +45 % affinity, +11 % purchase intent
  against showing it later. *(TikTok Creative Center, "Power of Creative Elements")*
- **The hook is decided within about 6 s** (VidMob, 1,678 TikTok ads, 7.3 bn impressions):
  real people hook 1.7× more often than celebrities; direct-to-camera shots lift 2-second
  view-through by 14 %.
- **Front-load**: 6 s of viewing delivered 38 % of the recall of 20 s+ (MediaScience,
  343 participants).
- **Text on screen is near-universal** (86 % of ads use text boxes); captions +58 % recall.
  The "5–10 words a second" figure is flicker captions, not a headline meant to be read.
- **CTA cards**: +45 % recall, +19 % likeability. **Transitions/effects**: +53 % recall;
  "seamless" transitions +14 % view time — no source says what share of cuts should carry one.
- **Sound on**: 88 % of TikTok users call sound vital; Google's ABCD framework (audio is its
  "Connection" pillar) reports a +30 % short-term / +17 % long-term lift for the full
  approach. *(TikTok blog; Google/Kantar, 2021)*
- **Length**: agencies converge on 9–15 s for direct-response TikTok ads (practitioner
  guidance, no controlled study behind the cutoffs). *(Cometly)*

### Pacing data

- **Feature films**: average shot length fell from ~8–11 s (1930s) to ~4.3–4.9 s recently
  (Barry Salt's measurements over 15,000+ films, as consistently reported). **By genre** (Stephen Follows, 1997–2016): action 4.0 s, adventure 5.1 s,
  sci-fi 6.2 s, horror 15.7 s.
- **TV commercials got faster through the 1980s**, and more shots went with *lower* recall
  and persuasion (MacLachlan & Logan, J. Advertising Research 1993). The paper itself is
  blocked everywhere; the direction is corroborated by several summaries, **the "under 2 s
  a shot by 1991" number is not**.
- **Trailers** average 114.2 s (≈20,000 trailers, 2000–2016). A 2026 paper on automatic
  trailer editing rejects a fixed cut-per-beat model: real trailer cutting is "elastic" —
  fast cuts on energetic passages, sustained shots on quiet bars (arXiv 2605.27067).
- **Luxury**: Chanel's *No. 5 the Film* (2004) is one continuous 180 s narrative with a
  swelling orchestral score and no hit; Dior's *J'adore — The Future Is Gold* (2014) is one
  uninterrupted rising action to a single reveal; Old Spice's *The Man Your Man Could Smell
  Like* (2010) is a 33 s single take. Zero cuts is a brand choice, not a constraint.

### Wedding films

- **Forms**: teaser **60–90 s**, highlight film 5–7 min (3–10 by package). One documented
  real highlight (Rowe Films, *Kerry & Oli*) ran about 12 min. *(One Story Weddings,
  Sven Studios, Rowe Films)*
- **Shot lengths**: 6–10 s holds for highlight moments, 1–2 s cuts in the faster montage,
  cut to a 90–110 BPM track. *(Sven Studios — one guide, quoted verbatim)*
- **Slow motion** at 60 fps on a 24/25 fps timeline (about 2.5×) for the kiss, vows and
  reactions. *(One Click Events)*
- **Endings**: fade to black and a names-and-date card is the described norm; **no source
  gives a duration** for either. The one ending documented in sound detail resolves a riser
  into a hit exactly on the blackout — not silence. *(Rowe Films)*
- **Grade varies**: warm and "cinematic but real" (The Wedding Filmer), moody desaturated
  (a 2025 viral film), fully black-and-white (a Fstoppers feature).

## What was struck — do not cite these

The fact-checkers caught invented or wrong figures; each looked plausible:

| claim | why struck |
|---|---|
| *You Can't Stop Us* averages 1.25 s a shot | 90 ÷ 72 — but the 72 clips play as 36 simultaneous pairs: 2.5 s |
| its editors "tested 900+ combinations" | on none of the cited pages |
| *1984*'s storyboard was "14 drawings plus a faded 15th" | on neither cited page |
| *Welcome Home* is scored to an FKA Twigs track | the song is Anderson .Paak's; FKA Twigs dances |
| e.l.f. #eyeslipsface drove "5B+ views, 3M+ videos" | its own release says ~1 bn views and ~400,000 videos |
| "63 % of top TikTok ads front-load the message"; "sound-off ads are 15 % more likely skipped"; "music in 63 %, voice-over in 39 %" | not on the cited pages as fetched |
| TV spots averaged "under 2 s a shot by 1991" | the paper cannot be opened; only the direction is corroborated |
| trailers hold set-up shots "2–4 s" and use a braam at the turn | the cited pages give lengths only (a braam *is* standard trailer vocabulary — just not from these sources) |
| a B&W wedding film runs "5 minutes" and paces so we can "digest what I'm watching" | both from a YouTube commenter, not the article |
| the Kiara–Sidharth film's song cut drew 58M views | only the film's 18M is on the page |
| J/L-cuts under ducked music are "described as standard" by the cited guides | neither guide mentions them; one real film does it |

## What it means for the recipes

Confidence is how directly the evidence bears on the number, not how sure the idea is.

> **Applied the same day, #1–#6, with the user's OK.** As built: pacing is in
> seconds (`Recipe.pacing`), converted at each recipe's own tempo so Wedding
> highlight and Product reveal design the same ad at their own tempo as before;
> Energy 2.0 → 0.9 s; Trailer 2.4 s → 1 s → 0.9 s in its last act; Fashion
> 4.5–7 s with `maxStretch: 2`; Wedding `defaultSeconds: 60` (the length is
> set by the recipe EXPECTED before the model answers — the pinned one, or the
> tone's from the brief's words); the playbook's hook rule. **Not in the
> table, needed to make #2 true:** a per-recipe `shortestSeconds` (Energy and
> Trailer 0.75 s — two beats at 124 BPM), so a crowded ad leaves a picture
> out rather than squeezing shots onto every beat; for a clip it is capped at
> the clip's footage. The stricter floor exposed an older engine bug (a hero
> clip held at its footage with every other shot at its shortest was accepted
> into a window too small for them) — fixed. #3's "elastic" mode is later.

| # | recipe · field | now | proposal | evidence | confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | all · pacing units | beats | **seconds, snapped to beats** — the shot a recipe designs is the same length at 90 and 160 BPM; the engine already snaps every boundary to a beat | our own `cutPlan.ts` rule ("cut density should be tempo-invariant"), `AUTOMATION.md` §5b; the recipes break it | high (our rule; the web adds nothing against it) |
| 2 | energy · pacing | 2 → 0.5 beats (0.97 → 0.24 s at 124 BPM; the 0.4 s floor makes the second half a cut every beat) | **about 2.0 s → 0.9 s** — never a shot every beat; the drive comes from hits, whooshes, moments and the grid treatment ON the beats in between (C3, C4) | fastest verified montage 2.5 s/cut (Nike); more shots → lower recall (M&L, direction); our own CapCut measurement: the fast template changes a *treatment* four times a beat over continuous footage, its shots run on; `cutPlan.ts`: never cut on every beat | medium–high |
| 3 | trailer · act three | 0.5 beat (0.3 s at 100 BPM — the 0.4 s floor rounds it up to a whole beat, 0.6 s: a cut every beat) | **about 0.9 s**, same reasoning; and later, an "elastic" mode where shot length follows the music's energy at that point (the engine has `energyAt`) | as #2; arXiv 2605.27067 | medium |
| 4 | fashion · pacing | 5–7 beats (3.3–4.7 s at 90 BPM) | **about 4.5–7 s**, longest in the middle; and let a fashion ad with few pictures hold longer rather than end early (a per-recipe stretch limit above 1.5) | Chanel, Dior, Old Spice — all near-zero cuts | high (direction), low (exact seconds) |
| 5 | wedding · default length | the brief's default, 30 s | **60 s** when the music allows — the teaser form; the 3–7 min highlight film stays out of the ad-maker | One Story Weddings, Sven Studios | high |
| 6 | product-reveal, trailer · early brand cue | the product is named on the hook card only in the standard cut; the model may leave it off | **the playbook asks for the product or brand on the hook's card**, inside the first 2.5 s; the end card stays | TikTok Creative Center (+48 % recall for a title in 2–2.5 s) | medium |
| 7 | wedding · hero floor 6 s, faces ×1.5, tempo 90 | — | **no change** — 6 s is the floor of the 6–10 s holds the one guide gives; 90 BPM its lower edge | Sven Studios | medium |
| 8 | energy · 5 cards, 4 hits | — | **no change — do not raise** | M&L direction; Nike's one closing line | medium |
| 9 | all · silence before the black | — | **keep as the default**; offer "a hit into the black" as a C3 option (weddings especially) | *1984* (near-silence) vs *Kerry & Oli* (riser into a hit on blackout) — one each | medium |
| 10 | all · black length, end-card seconds | 1–4 beats; 2–3 s | **no change, and no source** — these stay our guesses until the blind ratings tune them | no source gives a duration | — |
| 11 | wedding · look | warm-film | no change to the default; a wedding may want moody or B&W — worth a look choice later | three real films, three grades | low |
| 12 | all · minimum shot 0.4 s | — | no change (with #2 and #3 no recipe designs near it) | Nike 2.5 s | high |

What the research **cannot** tell us: exact seconds for the black and the end card, how many
cards is right, and whether our curves read as designed rather than automatic. That is what
the blind ratings are for (`npm run eval:rate`) — the eval now renders spine@2 ads with the
grade and the ending in them, so the ratings measure the recipes as they are.
